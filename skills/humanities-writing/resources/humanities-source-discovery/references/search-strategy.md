# Search strategy for humanities research

Scale the search to its use. A short reading pathway needs credible starting points;
a field survey needs broader coverage. Start with supplied bibliographies and
accessible source files before asking the user to reproduce context.

Develop terms from the research question: original-language concepts, historical
vocabulary, translations, variant names, neighboring debates, and critical terms.
Avoid letting a single contemporary English category define the field in advance.

Use review essays and reference works for orientation; locate representative
monographs, articles, chapters, editions, and dissertations where relevant. Trace
citations backward to formulations and forward to revisions or objections.
Inspect the material needed to support the account, not just search snippets.

Change queries when results reveal different categories, periods, or disciplinary
framings. Search for credible contrary interpretations as well as supporting work.
Include regional and non-English scholarship when central to the question and
disclose language or access limits.

Retain useful retrieval notes for a substantial survey: query families, databases,
coverage, important exclusions, and inspected works. Do not generate a formal search
log for every lookup unless requested or needed for a reproducible review.

Stop when additional searching no longer changes the relevant positions, source
bases, or answer at the requested scale. Expand only for an identified consequential
omission; do not equate saturation with exhaustive knowledge of the field.
