# Digital Humanities and Mixed-Method Paper Template

## Working agreement

The user's current request and established authorization take precedence over
this skill's workflow defaults. Use the available context, resolve routine choices,
and complete the requested work. Ask only about missing information that materially
changes meaning, evidence, scope, or authorization; continue independent work.
Treat later corrections as steering unless the user replaces the task.

Keep supporting guides subordinate to that scope. Their examples and suggested
structures are optional. If a skill requirement actually blocks delivery, identify
the file and exact clause and explain the affected part; do not infer a new approval
requirement from a recommendation.

## Boundary

This is a special-purpose bridge between humanities inquiry and technical method.
It must not turn a humanities question into a method demonstration or treat
computational output as self-interpreting evidence.

For ordinary humanities paper planning, use humanities-research. For a dataset,
benchmark, digital edition, or evaluation resource, consider
benchmark-paper-template.

## Core design

Build the paper around these linked layers:

1. Scholarly problem: what historical, interpretive, cultural, philosophical, or
   religious-studies problem requires attention?
2. Existing understanding: what do current interpretations, sources, or methods
   reveal and obscure?
3. Corpus or evidence base: how were materials selected, delimited, digitised, and
   represented?
4. Technical operation: what transformation, model, code, or measurement is used
   and why is it appropriate to the question?
5. Validation: how are OCR, annotation, categories, model output, uncertainty, and
   errors checked against sources or expert judgment?
6. Interpretive analysis: how do computational patterns return to close reading,
   historical context, field evidence, or conceptual argument?
7. Contribution: what changes in scholarly understanding, and what technical or
   resource contribution is genuinely reusable?
8. Limits and ethics: what is excluded, distorted, inaccessible, sensitive, or
   non-generalizable?

The technical method serves the scholarly problem. If the paper's only contribution
is faster or more accurate computation with no humanities intervention, use the
legacy technical references instead.

Do not call a technically competent result a scholarly contribution unless it
changes an existing interpretation or makes a consequential question newly
answerable.

## Consistency checks

- Problem-method fit: computation enables an answer that the source base and
  question warrant.
- Corpus-claim fit: selection, survival, digitisation, language, and metadata biases
  bound the interpretation.
- Transformation transparency: OCR, cleaning, normalisation, tokenisation,
  translation, classification, and exclusions are documented.
- Validation-claim fit: performance metrics do not substitute for interpretive
  validity.
- Pattern-reading loop: aggregate findings are checked against primary materials
  and plausible alternative explanations.
- Contribution honesty: the paper distinguishes scholarly, methodological,
  technical, and resource contributions.
- Reproducibility and ethics: code, parameters, derived data, permissions, privacy,
  and community risk are addressed appropriately.

A problem-method or corpus-claim mismatch is serious when it undermines a central
claim; distinguish that failure from a local issue that can be repaired.

## Structure

Do not force a fixed section order. A common structure is:

- Introduction: scholarly problem, intervention, materials, and why the technical
  operation is necessary.
- Scholarship and methodological context.
- Corpus, provenance, digitisation, and ethics.
- Technical method and reproducible pipeline.
- Validation and error analysis.
- Interpretive findings organised by research question.
- Return to representative or discrepant sources.
- Discussion, limitations, and scholarly contribution.
- Conclusion.

The interpretive findings should not be reduced to a Results section detached from
historical or textual analysis.

## Output

For a full planning request, select the useful parts below. For a narrower request,
produce that deliverable without a compulsory audit or full template.

Possible components:

1. project type and why this template applies;
2. one-sentence scholarly problem and tentative intervention;
3. corpus/evidence rationale;
4. method and transformation ledger;
5. validation plan;
6. interpretive return plan;
7. section skeleton;
8. consistency failures and next actions.

Draft prose when requested; otherwise deliver the requested plan or structural advice.
Stop after checks appropriate to the actual contribution and task are complete.
