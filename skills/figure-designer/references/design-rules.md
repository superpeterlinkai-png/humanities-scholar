# Figure design by material and purpose

Apply the rules relevant to the actual visual, output format, and final display size.
Visual fidelity, editability, and interpretation may call for different media.

## Formats

Use editable vector text, shapes, and connectors for diagrams where possible.
Use raster images for photographs, scans, facsimiles, textures, and detailed artwork.
Mixed figures may combine both. Select resolution for the intended size and
inspection needs; do not claim that a vector wrapper adds detail to a blurry image.

Preserve source wording and material evidence. Do not trace a historical photograph
or inscription into simplified shapes merely to satisfy a vector-only preference.
Follow an actual venue or user format requirement when specified and verified.

## Legibility and encoding

Inspect at the intended size. Use readable fonts, adequate contrast, sufficient
space, and clear grouping. Keep meaningful scripts, diacritics, uncertainty marks,
and captions legible. Do not use color as the only carrier of a necessary distinction.

Labels and legends should explain the relation represented. Arrows may imply
direction, influence, or causation; choose and label them accordingly. Distinguish
documented, inferred, contested, and unknown relationships when the difference matters.

Captions should identify the visual's purpose, scope, evidence, and any crucial
construction choices. A photograph or conceptual diagram need not announce an
experimental finding in its first sentence.

## Quantitative figures only

Name units and denominators. Choose scales according to what the marks encode:
bar lengths ordinarily need a zero baseline; a restricted line or scatter range
can be appropriate when clearly labeled and not misleading. Explain transformations,
logarithmic scales, uncertainty intervals, missingness, and aggregation where relevant.

Highlight evidence relevant to the question, not an author's preferred method at
the expense of honest comparison. Avoid decorative 3D or distorted area encodings.

## Final inspection

Inspect the actual exported or displayed output for clipping, overlap, missing
glyphs, altered relations, image quality, and correspondence with the prose.
Structural checks do not establish visual quality. Recheck affected outputs after
corrections; finish when required fidelity and usability are met or limitations
are accurately reported.
