# Check a resource or evaluation paper before submission

Use the items relevant to the actual contribution and target venue. This is not
a fixed paper structure or a requirement to add experiments to a scholarly edition.

- The scholarly question and intended users are clear.
- Source provenance, selection, exclusions, and rights are traceable.
- Categories and annotations are justified, with uncertainty and disagreement preserved.
- Collection and transformations are documented at a useful level.
- Validation addresses relevant errors and scholarly usefulness.
- Actual use cases or evaluations support the claimed contribution.
- Limits, bias, access, versioning, and maintenance are stated where relevant.
- Tables, figures, claims, and available artifacts agree.

For a machine-learning benchmark, additionally assess task definition, suitable
baselines, data separation, contamination, metrics, and fair comparison as applicable.
Do not invent results or require a companion model without a research reason.

Verify current official requirements for the exact venue, track, and submission
year. Data documentation formats, mandatory sections, anonymity, licensing, and
artifact rules can change. An inherited claim that a particular metadata format
has been mandatory since a fixed year is not a sufficient authority.

Prioritize failures by effect: an invalid central inference or untraceable source
is more serious than a missing preferred chart or an optional “Finding” summary.
Finish once actual requirements and consequential issues have been addressed.
