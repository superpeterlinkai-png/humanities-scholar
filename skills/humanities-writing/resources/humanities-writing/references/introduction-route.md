# Choose the introduction's argumentative route

Start from the genre, available evidence, and intended claim. Possible openings
include a conceptual tension, source passage, historical puzzle, interpretive
dispute, or normative problem. Use a real example only when it earns its space.

Decide what the reader needs in order to understand why the question matters and
how the paper answers it. Context, scholarship, the question, thesis, sources,
method, stakes, and scope can appear in different orders or be combined.

A tentative introduction may expose a choice still under investigation. State
the status clearly rather than inventing a mature intervention. Do not require
six paragraphs, a running example, experiments, or an engineering problem-solution arc.

For a restructuring request, use the existing material to implement an improved
sequence. Preserve voice, citation integrity, and the strength of the claim.
