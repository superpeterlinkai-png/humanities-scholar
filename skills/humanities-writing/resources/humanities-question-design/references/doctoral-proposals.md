# Developing a doctoral proposal

Use for a doctoral research design or substantive revision. This supplements
research judgment; it is not a mandatory outline or a demand that an application
already contain a completed dissertation.

## Carry earlier work into a new problem

Preserve the training and live question in an earlier thesis while checking its
inferences independently. Explain briefly what the next project can now investigate.
Avoid making the proposal a long confession of previous mistakes. Correcting an
inference, separating concepts, or rejecting an essentialist account may open the
inquiry without completing its doctoral contribution.

Develop the positive task: what account, interpretation, explanation, or argument
will the research attempt, and what would make the alternatives consequentially
different? A working answer can be provisional. If the old thesis moves from how
something is experienced to what kind of object it is, locate the missing support
and decide whether the new project supplies it or deliberately narrows its question.
Merely renaming the levels does not repair the move.

## Demonstrate traction at the proposal's scale

Inspect enough relevant scholarship now to situate the intended intervention.
Check whether the advertised novelty is already an established point. Do not claim
an exhaustive gap or postpone all scholarly positioning to the first year.
Where helpful, develop one short analysis, source relation, or argument that shows
how the proposed method bears on the difficulty. Explain why the chosen materials
can discriminate between accounts; naming a corpus alone does not do this.

Keep the pilot's status clear. An invented variation tests an argument; a reader's
possible response is not an observed population pattern; a passage does not stand
for an entire tradition. Countercases should threaten or refine the proposed answer
where relevant, rather than decorate every proposal. A philosophical design can be
feasible without fieldwork, experiments, or a large literary corpus.

Make the work plan follow dependencies: what must be understood before a later
claim can be attempted? Describe a feasible scope and material or language needs.
Avoid assigning generic reading, analysis, and writing to three interchangeable
years or promising unearned language competence.

## Adapt to the actual programme

Verify current official instructions when preparing an application. Distinguish
page limits, word limits, and what the limit includes; record the counting convention
when the rule is ambiguous and use a defensible conservative interpretation.
Prioritize the department's applicable rule over generic university guidance.

Tailoring should explain why the programme can support this question and method,
not just substitute supervisor names. Separate intellectual fit, admission
eligibility, and supervision availability. An official staff profile can establish
research interests, not willingness or agreement to supervise. Flag a consequential
eligibility issue in the accompanying advice while completing the authorized draft.
Do not invent contact, endorsement, competence, or experience.

## Deliver the commissioned proposal

Write a coherent prospective inquiry in the applicant's voice, with the evidence
strength the materials support. Use headings and bibliography proportionate to the
actual limit. Keep drafting notes and access limitations outside the application
unless they matter to its research claims. A polished proposal can remain a proposed
direction for the user to judge; do not report that the user has endorsed every
new scholarly commitment merely because drafting was authorized.
